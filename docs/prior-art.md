# Prior art

Loadbearing builds on well-established ideas. This page names them, says exactly how Loadbearing differs from each, and lists documented real-world failures of the shape it is built to catch. Every source below is linked so it can be checked.

## What it builds on

| Work | What it does | How Loadbearing differs |
|---|---|---|
| **Metamorphic testing.** T. Y. Chen, S. C. Cheung and S. M. Yiu, *Metamorphic Testing: A New Approach for Generating Next Test Cases*, HKUST technical report HKUST-CS98-01, 1998 ([arXiv:2002.12543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12543)). Survey: S. Segura, G. Fraser, A. B. Sánchez and A. Ruiz-Cortés, "A Survey on Metamorphic Testing", *IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering* 42(9), 2016 ([record](https://hdl.handle.net/11441/38271)). | Finds faults without a test oracle, by checking relations between the outputs of several runs on transformed inputs. | Loadbearing's relations are per input field and come from intent: destroying a field that should matter must move the output, and nudging a field that should not matter must not. Each run starts from the same byte-identical checkpoint, so the transformation is the only difference. |
| **Leave-one-feature-out.** D. Sculley et al., "Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems", NeurIPS 2015 ([paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2015/file/86df7dcfd896fcaf2674f757a2463eba-Paper.pdf)). | Names *underutilized data dependencies*, "input signals that provide little incremental modeling benefit", and says: "Underutilized dependencies can be detected via exhaustive leave-one-feature-out evaluations. These should be run regularly to identify and remove unnecessary features." | The same instinct, applied to any running pipeline rather than to a model's training features, and turned around: Loadbearing also flags the field a pipeline should use but silently does not, and the field it should ignore but reacts to. |
| **Input erasure.** J. Li, W. Monroe and D. Jurafsky, "Understanding Neural Networks through Representation Erasure", 2016 ([arXiv:1612.08220](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.08220)). | Explains a neural model's decisions by erasing parts of its input or internal representation and measuring the effect. | Works from outside the pipeline, on whole input fields, with no access to model internals, and compares what moves against what was supposed to move. |
| **Global sensitivity analysis.** A. Saltelli et al., *Global Sensitivity Analysis: The Primer*, Wiley, 2008 (ISBN 978-0-470-05997-5, [doi:10.1002/9780470725184](https://doi.org/10.1002/9780470725184)). | Apportions the variation in a model's output to its inputs, including interactions between inputs. | Loadbearing changes one field at a time and gives a yes-or-no verdict per field, not a sensitivity index. It therefore does not measure interactions between inputs, which global methods do. |
| **How LLMs use their context.** N. F. Liu et al., "Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts", *Transactions of the ACL* 12, 2024, pp. 157-173 ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/)). | Shows that language models do not robustly use information in long contexts: performance drops when the relevant passage sits in the middle. | This is why a retrieval pipeline can answer fluently without using what it retrieved. Loadbearing checks, for one deployed pipeline, whether the retrieved field actually moves the answer. |

## What the combination adds

1. **A verdict per field, tied to intent.** Intent is declared in the target, or inferred from the pipeline's own docs with a verbatim quote and its link.
2. **Byte-identical forks.** Every run starts from one checkpoint, a Nebius Token Factory Sandboxes snapshot or, on the local backend, a copied directory, so the mutated field is the only difference between runs.
3. **A noise floor.** LLM output varies on its own; keys that move with no mutation are measured and left out, instead of voiding the report.
4. **A proven fix.** The fix for a defect is applied in a fresh fork of the same checkpoint and the probe is re-run, so "the defect is gone and nothing else broke" is shown, not claimed.

## Real failures of this shape

These cases are not claims about how each system was built. They show the failure Loadbearing targets: confident, well-formed output that does not reflect the input it was given.

- **Air Canada's website chatbot (2022, decided 2024).** A customer asked about bereavement fares and was told he could apply for the discount after travelling, which the airline's own policy did not allow. The Civil Resolution Tribunal of British Columbia held the airline responsible for what its chatbot said and ordered it to pay. *Moffatt v. Air Canada*, 2024 BCCRT 149, decided 2024-02-14 ([decision](https://decisions.civilresolutionbc.ca/crt/crtd/en/525448/1/document.do), [report](https://mobilesyrup.com/2024/02/16/air-canada-has-to-pay-for-chatbot-mistake/)).
- **New York City's MyCity business chatbot (2024).** An investigation found it telling business owners, among other things, that landlords need not accept tenants on rental assistance and that stores may refuse cash, both contrary to city law ([The Markup, 2024-03-29](https://themarkup.org/news/2024/03/29/nycs-ai-chatbot-tells-businesses-to-break-the-law)).
- **Public Health England's case reporting (October 2020).** Nearly 16,000 positive COVID-19 cases from 2020-09-25 to 2020-10-02 were missed from the daily figures. The reported cause: templates in the older XLS format, which hold only around 65,000 rows, filled up, so later results were not added and went unreported, and contact tracing for those cases was delayed ([The Conversation, 2020-10-07](https://theconversation.com/why-you-should-never-use-excel-to-count-coronavirus-cases-147681)).
